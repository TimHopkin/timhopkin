SDG 15: Life on Land - The Foundation of Global Resilience

Why protecting, restoring, and promoting sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems is the bedrock of our future economy

"Protect, restore and promote sustainable use of terrestrial ecosystems, sustainably manage forests, combat desertification, and halt and reverse land degradation and halt biodiversity loss."

The Criticality of SDG 15

Sustainable Development Goal 15 (SDG 15) is not just an environmental target; it is the foundation upon which our economic and social stability rests. 'Life on Land' encompasses the health of the soil, the purity of our water, the air we breathe, and the biodiversity that sustains all life systems.

Without healthy terrestrial ecosystems, we cannot hope to achieve climate resilience, food security, or economic prosperity. The degradation of land is a direct threat to global GDP and human wellbeing.

Connecting to the Land Economy

For the land economy to thrive, we must align our practices with the targets of SDG 15. This means moving beyond extraction to regeneration. It requires a fundamental shift in how we value land—not just as a physical asset, but as a living system that provides essential services.

Digital land management plays a crucial role here. By mapping and monitoring our natural capital, we can make informed decisions that halt degradation and actively restore ecosystems while maintaining productive capacity.

A Call for Integrated Action

Achieving SDG 15 requires integrated action across sectors. Governments, private finance, and land managers must collaborate to create frameworks that reward sustainable stewardship. We need transparent supply chains and spatially targeted interventions that direct resources to where they can have the greatest impact on biodiversity and soil health.
